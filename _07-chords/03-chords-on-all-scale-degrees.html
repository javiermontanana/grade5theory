We have already seen that a major triad can be seen as the tonic triad of the corresponding major key, and the minor triad can be seen as the tonic triad of the corresponding minor key. Said another way: in a major key, the tonic triad will be a major triad, and in a minor key, the tonic triad will be a minor triad.

In this section we will form triads on all the other degrees of the scale in major and minor keys. I will use C major and C minor as an example, but you should try doing the same thing in other keys.

Labelling

Most of the time, when we identify chords going forward, we will think of them within the context of a key. So if we are in, say, C major, a C major chord would be the tonic chord, and a G major chord would be the dominant chord. To label chords, we will use roman numerals and put these below the chords, so the C major chord would be chord I, and the G major chord, chord V. As the roman numerals only make sense in a key, we will always name the key as part of our labelling.
