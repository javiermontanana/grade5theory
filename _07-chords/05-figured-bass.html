--- layout: chapter title: Figured Bass ---

Figured bass is a system where numbers are used to indicate the chords above a bass line. It developed in the Baroque period as part of a practice called basso continuo, where performers would improvise an accompaniment to a main instrument. The composer would write the bass line of the accompaniment, plus some numbers to indicate the chords, and the performer would interpret those and make up a full accompaniment.

The numbers in figured bass correspond to the intervals from the bass to the other notes in the chord. So a root position chord is five-three ( 5
3 ), a first inversion chord is six-three ( 6
3 ), and a second inversion chord is six-four ( 6
4 ) .

In practice the figures for root position chords are often omitted, and the 3 in 6
3 chords is also often omitted.

Figured bass gets much more complex than this, but what we have seen here is enough for describing triads and their inversions. This provides a different way of labelling chords to what we learnt in the previous section. Instead of using roman numerals and letters, we can use roman numerals and figured bass. In the grade 5 exam, you need to know and use the system with letters, not figured bass, but it is good to start to become familiar with it, as it is much more widely used and you are likely to encounter it in pieces from the Baroque period.
