Chord tones and Non-Chord Tones

In the examples above, every single note in the melody was a member of the corresponding chord. But this is not always the case. Melodies often use notes that are not memebers of the chord (non-chord tones or embelishment notes).

In western classical music, there are some rules that composers tend to follow about how non-chord tones may be used. In this section we are going to learn some of those rules. The rules laid out here are incomplete and oversimplified, but they are a good starting point and they will serve us well enough to answer the questions in the grade 5 theory exam. If you wish, this is a topic that you can study in much more depth.

Broadly speaking, melodies can move between chord notes without restriction. Melodies can jump between chord tones. Non-chord tones may be used to connect other chord tones by step.

Finding Suitable Chords for a Melody

In the grade 5 theory exam, you will need to answer a question where you are given a short melody and you need to suggest suitable chords to fit.

You will just see the melody and you'll need to decide which chords fit and give the Roman numerals for those chords. Remember that the only chords you should use in the grade 5 theory exam are I, II, IV and V.

Of course, the chord and non-chord tones won't be labelled, so you need to decide which are which. This process involves using your intuition and possibly some trial and error. Most non-chord tones will be either passing or auxiliary notes. Very occasionally, there may be other types of non-chord tones, in which case you may need to guess. But in any case, in the melodies given in the exam, most of the notes will be the chord tones.
