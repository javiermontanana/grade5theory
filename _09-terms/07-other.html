Pause

The sign 𝄐 is called a pause or fermata (Italian for "stop"). It is placed above a note (or rest) to indicate that it should be held for longer than its normal duration.

attacca

The word attacca is used at the end of a section or movement of a work, and it means that the next section should follow without a pause.

8va and loco

In the chapter on scales and registers, in the section on the grand staff we saw the meaning of the 8ve signs and loco.

Similarly

The word simile (meaning "similar") and the phrase come prima (meaning "like the first time") are used to indicate that something should be played with the performance direction that was given previously.

For example, a composer mgiht write staccato dots for a few notes at the begging of a piece and then the word simile, suggesting that the music should continue staccato.

Spread chord

A wavy line next to a chord, ( 𝆃 ) means that it should be spread, which means that instead of playing all the notes at exactly the same time, the notes should be played one after another in quick succession, starting with the bottom note and finishing with the top note.

This is common in music for piano and harp.

Alla breve

The phrase alla breve means that the beats are minims (rather than the more common crotchets). Cut time rather than Common time is an example of this (See the relevant section in the Rhythm and Metre chapter. ).
