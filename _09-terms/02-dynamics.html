Basic dynamics

The two most basic words to indicate dynamics are forte and piano, which in Italian mean strong/loud and soft/quiet. These are often abbreviated to the letters f and p.

The word mezzo which means medium/moderately can be added before the words forte or piano. Thus mezzoforte or mf is a medium-loud dynamic and mezzopiano or mp is a medium quiet one.

ff stands for fortissimo, an extremely loud dynamic and pp stands for pianissimo an extremely quiet one. The Italian suffix -issimo means extremely.

We can put all the above together to form all the basic dynamics in order from quietest to loudest.

Gradual changes

To indicate that the music should gradually get louder and louder, the word crescendo or cresc. is used (Italian for growing).

To indicate that the music should get quieter and quieter, the words diminuendo or decrescendo are used, often abbreviated to dim. or decresc. meaning getting smaller.

Instead of these words, hairpin signs can also be used.

Other dynamic instructions

The words morendo, perdendosi, and smorzando all mean dying or fading away. These imply the music should become quieter, and may also suggest a slowing down.

The phrase a niente means “to nothing”, implying that the sound should fade away until it disappears completely.

Rinforzando (often abbreviated rinf. or rf) means reinforcing, indicating a strengthening of the sound.

The word sonoro means resonant or full-sounding, suggesting a rich, projecting tone.

The phrase sotto voce means “under the voice”, indicating that the music should be played quietly and restrained.
