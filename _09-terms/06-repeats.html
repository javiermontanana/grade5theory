Often large sections of music need to be repeated. In these cases, rather than having to write out everything twice, there are certain sign we can use.

Repeat signs

Back in chapter 1, we learnt what a final barline is. A final barline with two dots on the left (𝄇) is a repeat sign. It tells the performer that they should repeat the music.

The mirror image of that sign (𝄆) tells the performer where to start from when they repeat. If this sign is absent, then they should start again from the beginning.

D.C. and D. S. and fine

The Italian words Da Capo or Dal Segno can be used instead. These are often abbreviated to D.C or D.S. and they mean "from the beginnng" and "from the sign" respectively. The "sing" that dal segno refers to is this one: 𝄋.

D.C. tells the performer they should start again from the beginning and D.S. tells them they should start again from wherever 𝄋 is.

These words are ofen used together with the word fine which means "end." The word fine will often be found somewhere in the middle of the music. If you see the instruction D.C. al fine at the end of the music, it means that when you get to the end, you should start again from the beginning and play until fine. first and second time bars

Sometimes a large section of music will be repeated, except that there is some change at the end. In this case we can use a repeat sign with first time bar and a second time bar. These look like a box above the music with a nummber 1 or a number 2. They are also referred to by their italian names, prima volta ("first time") and seconda volta ("second time.")

Repeated notes whose value is of a quaver or shorter can be indicated by writing a longer note value and diagonal lines above the note, or through the stem, to shew the note values that should be played. When the note value of the written note is crotchets or longer, the number of lines corresponds to the number of flags of the note value of the repeated notes. attacca and fermata

The word attacca at the end of a section or movement means that the next section should follow immediately without a pause.

The sign 𝄐 indicates a pause. It is called a fermata (Italian for "stop"). It is usually found above a note or rest, and it means that that it should be elongated.
