--- layout: chapter title: Grace Notes ---

Grace notes are notes printed in a small font. They are ornaments so their value is not couted towards the total duration of a bar.

Grace notes can take many forms but in the grade 5 theory exam, you encounter two kinds: the appoggiatura and the acciaccatura.

Both the appoggiatura and the acciaccatura are a single grace note, which is connected (and often slurred) to a main note. They look very similar, the difference is that the acciaccatura has a line going through it.

The acciaccatura is performed faster than the appoggiatura. As an approximate rule of thumb, we could say that the appoggiatura is given a duration of the note value it is written as, or that it "steals" about half of the duration of the main note, whereas the acciaccatura is played as quickly as possible.

Name Written Performed
Appoggiatura
Acciaccatura

The word appoggiatura comes from the Italian word for leaning, so the sound should convey a sense of leaning on that note. The word acciaccatura comes from the Italian word for crushing or sqaushing, which is conveyed by it being played very fast.
