--- layout: chapter title: Transposing at the octave and changing clef ---

Note: to understand this section, you need to know how the various clefs work, so you might need to read through the chapter on clefs and registers first.

Sometimes we will need to transpose something by one or more octaves, and change the clef at the same time. There is nothing new you need to learn in order to be able to do this. You just need to know what transposing is, and you need to know your clefs and registers.

Transposing at the octave is simpler than other intervals (since the key signature and the note-names won't change) but it is very easy to make a mistake and end up in the wrong octave when changing clefs. So make sure you have a reference, say middle C, that you can locate in both clefs, and from there make sure you count the number of octaves correctly.
