String insturments are ones where the sound is produced by making a string vibrate. 

In woodwind and brass insturments, the performer blows air into the instrument 
and the vibrations of the air inside the insturment produce the sound. 
Woodwind instruments are often made of wood, and brass instruments are often made of metal/brass, but not always!
The essential difference between the two types is that in brass insturments, 
the performer's lips vibrate in the pouthpiece. 

In percussion instruments, the sound is produced by stiking the insturment.

# string instruments

## bowed strings

These are the insturments in the violin family.

In order from the highest sounding to the lowest they are: the violin, the viola, the cello and the double bass. 

They each have 4 strings, and are mainly played with a bow (though they can also be played in other ways.)


### range and clefs

The highest note these insturments can produce is not concretely defined, 
but they all have a confortable range off at least 3 octaves. 
Their lowest notes and their main clefs are given in the table below. 

The double bass sounds an octave lower than written. 
The lowest note in the table below  is given at sounding (concert) pitch.

### performance directions and techniques

pizz and arco

To indicate that notes should be played by plucking the strings (rather than with the bow) 
the Italian word **pizzicato** or **pizz.** is used. 

To cancel this instruction and tell the performer to use the bow again, the word **arco** is used. 



#### bowing 

The bow can be used in 2 directions. 
String players call these down-bow and up-bow, and they use signs to indicate 
when a note should be played with a specific direction of the bow. 

As we saw in chapter 9, slurs mean legato, 
smooth connected playing. 

For bowed string instruments, 
slurs mean that the notes in the slur should be played in a single bow-stroke (in a single bown-bow or up-bow). 


#### mutes

These instruments can use mutes to dampen the sound.

#### string instructions 

To indicate that something should be played on a particular string, 
the composer can write **sul** followed by the letter-name of the string.
eg *sul G* (meaning "on the G")

The harp is a plucked string instrument. 
It has one string per note of the scale, 
so 7 strings per octave, corresponding to the 7 letter-names. 

To play sharps and flats, the harp has seven pedals, 
which can each be set to the natural, flat, or sharp position. 


It has a very wide range, reaching almonst as high and as low as a piano. 
Like the piano, the harp uses a grand staff (link)

# percussion insturments 

With percussion instruments, the sound is produced by striking the instrument. 

They are divided into two groups: 
- instruments that produce a definite pitch 
- instruments that produce an indefinite pitch 

## Percussion instruments that produce a definite pitch 

### timpani 

Timpani are a kind of drum (also called kettle-drums). 
The sound has a definite pitch. 
In order to play different notes, performers use several drums, 
or use a pedal to change the pitch.
