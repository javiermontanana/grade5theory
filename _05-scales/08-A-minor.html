--- layout: chapter title: Minor Scales - A minor ---

In the previous sections, we learnt about major scales. There is another kind of scale we need to learn about: minor scales.

Minor scales are a bit more complicated because there are 3 forms of the minor scale that we need to learn: natural, harmonic and melodic. Besides, as we will see, the melodic scale is different when it is going up to when it is going down.

The three types of minor scale have their own particular combination of tones and semitones (just like the major scales) but unlike what we did with the major scales, for the minor scales, we won't start by learning the pattern of tones and semitones. We will start by learning the 3 forms of A minor. Then, in the next section we will introduce all the other minor scales each with their key signature. In a later section, we will look at the patterns of tones and semitones in the minor scales and use them to write minor scales without key signatures.

A minor

A minor is the key with no sharps or flats in the key signature. In this section we will learn the 3 forms of A minor: natural, melodic and harmonic. In the next section we will learn all the other keys and their key signatures. The natural minor scale has the notes given by the key signature. To form the melodic and harmonic scales, we will start from the natural minor scale and we will raise some notes by a semitone with an accidental.

Natural minor

The natural minor scale is the most straightforward. We just use the notes given by the key signature, so in the case of A minor, all the notes are natural.

Harmonic minor

For the harmonic scale, we raise note 7 by a semitone going up and going down. In this case note 7 is a G natural, so it will become G sharp.

Melodic minor

For the melodic scale, we will raise notes 6 and 7 going up only.

So, as you can see, going down, the melodic minor scale is the same as the natural minor.
