--- layout: chapter title: What is a Key and What is a Scale ---

So far, we have been using the words key and scale without properly defining them. In this section we will go deeper into what these words mean and properly disinguish betweeen the two.

A scale is the set of notes ordered in stepwise ascending or descending order. By stepwise, in the case of major and minor scales, we mean that we will have one note for every line or space on the stave or, equivalently, one for every letter-name.

The key is the environment of the music, so that it will have the tonic as it's final arrival point. Music that is in a certain key will generally use notes form the scale(s) of that key, and for the purposes of the grade 5 exam, it is fine to think of keys in this way. But as you learn more about keys, you'll understand that this is not strictly speaking true, and what really defines the key of the music is what note is the music directed towards so that it finds a sense of closure on that note.

The example below is the second half of the song Happy Birthday It is in G major (by the way, this is the same example from section 7 earlier in the chapter). You can see (as we did earlier) that all the notes belong to G major. We have an F♯, C natural, etc

But try playing it on your instrument and try singing it. Doesn't it also feel like it has to end on the note G? G feels like the tonic, like the final point of closure of the music.

This is now in A major. You can check that all the notes belong to A major and that we have all the right sharps. But again, try to sing or play the tune with your instrument and you should feel that the music resolves at the tonic, which in this case is A.

What we have done here is something called transposing. We have transposed the song up a major 2nd (major 2nd is the interval from G to A) which just means that we have moved all the notes up by a major 2nd. We will learn more about transposing in a different chapter. I have only used it here to illustrate how the tonic is the final arrival point of the music and hopefully you are able to hear this by when you play/sing/hear the song in the two different keys.
