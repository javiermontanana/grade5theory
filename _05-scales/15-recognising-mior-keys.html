--- layout: chapter title: Recognising Minor Keys ---

When we were dealing with major keys, we could look at the notes in a melody, and see which major scale they belong to in order to identify the key. With minor keys, we will do a similar thing, but we need to bear in mind that the 6th and 7th scale degrees may be raised. However, as the raised 7th degree plays such an important role in defining the key, we should expect it to be raised at least once in the melody.

Besides identifying the relevant sharps and flats, and working out key signatures and raised 7ths, you should also try playing or singing the extracts and trying to get a feel for which note is the tonic, and whether the key is major or minor. In the case of minor keys, notice the significance of the raised leading note, and how, if you get rid of it, it loses the sense of arrival at the tonic. Notice also the role of the third degree. In minor keys this is a minor third above the tonic, and in major keys it is a major third above. Changing the third degree is often enough to turn a whole tune from major to minor or vice versa.

One of the skills you are tested on in aural tests is idetifying the key of a piece. Not by looking at the music, but by listening and using your intuition. As you learn the music theory side of this, you should also use you ears, your instrument and your voice to develop your sense for keys.
