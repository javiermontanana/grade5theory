--- layout: chapter title: Scale Degrees ---

So far, I have been referring to notes of the scale as note 1, note 2, note 3, etc. But this is a slightly improper way of referring to notes of the scale. In music theory we refer to degrees of the scale, or scale degrees. So for example, in G major, G would be scale degree 1, A is scale degree 2, F♯ is scale degree 7 and so on...

In the major and minor scales, each scale degree also has a name that you need to learn. Below is the list of all the names.

Scale Degree
Number 		Scale Degree
Name
1Tonic
2Supertonic
3Mediant
4Subdominant
5Dominant
6Submediant
7Leading Note or
Subtonic

By far the most important scale degrees are 1 and 5, the tonic and the dominant. The dominant is always a perfect fifth above the tonic.

Once we know these two, there are a few strategies which can help us remember the other ones.

The subdominant is scale degree 4. "Sub" means below, and the subdominant is in the same relation to the tonic as the dominant but from below. The subdominant is a perfect fifth below the tonic.

The mediant (from the latin for being in the middle) is midway between the tonic and the dominant, so it's scale degree 3.

The submediant is the "mediant" between the tonic and the subdominant, half way between the two—remember that the subdominant is a fifth below the tonic, so the submediant is a third below (scale degree 6).

The supertonic is scale degree 2. "Super" means above, and it is one step above the tonic.

Scale degree 7 is called the leading note when it is a semitone (minor 2nd) away from the tonic. The leading note has a tendency to pull (or "lead") to the tonic, as it is so close to it and the tonic is the final arrival point of the music. When it is a tone away from the tonic, scale degree 7 is called the subtonic, because it is one step below the tonic.
