Ornaments are melodic decorations which are added to a tune, indicated by the use of symbols or small-font notes. In this chapter we will learn about some types of ornaments, which you will need to be familiar with for the grade 5 theory exam.

We will learn the meaning behind the different ornament symbols, ie. how they should be performed, and we will show the interpretation of the ornament written out in the appropriate note values. But it is important to remember two things. Firstly, this written-out rhythm can only be an approximmation of what the ornament will sound like. Most notes in the ornament will be relatively fast, but shouldn't sound mathematical. Secondly, with ornaments, there is often a lot of room for interpretation, and different performers may choose to perform the same ornaments in very different ways, depending on their taste and preference. Furthermore, one may choose to perform the same type of ornament in a certain way in one context, and in a different way in another context.

Therefore, the written-out interpretations that we will see should only be taken as rough guidelines. In this chapter we will cover what you need to learn for the grade 5 theory exam, but, as a performer, you will develop your understanding and intuition for ornaments by encountering them in pieces. By seeing them in different musical contexts and making decisions about how to intertpret them, and by listening to other people perform their interpretations of them.

In the grade 5 theory exam, you will be asked to recognise the following types of ornaments:

  • trills
  • mordents
  • turns
  • acciaccaturas
  • appoggiaturas

In the following sections we will learn about each of these. Acciaccaturas and appoggiaturas are two examples of what we call grace notes, so they are under the section with that title.
