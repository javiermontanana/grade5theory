## chord types (quality)


When we form triads, we can we will be stacking major or minor thirds. 
As there are two thirds we need to stack and there are two kinds of third we can use, 
we can form four different types of chord. 

In this seciton we will learn about these chord types. 

Let's try to form the 4 different types on the note D. 

#### Major triad

If we have a major third and then a minor third on top, we have a major triad. 
A major triad has a perfect fifth between the root and the fifth. 

#### Minor triad

If we have a minor third and then a major third on top, we have a minor triad. 
A major triad has a perfect fifth between the root and the fifth. 

#### Diminished triad

If we have a minor third and then a minor third on top, we have a diminished triad. 
A diminished triad has a diminished fifth between the root and the fifth. 


#### Augmented triad 

If we have a major third and then a major third on top, we have an augmented triad. 
An augmented triad has an augmented fifth between the root and the fifth. 


Diminished and augmented triads get their name from the fifth. 
Major and minor triads have a perfect fifth, so they get their name from the third. 

One way to identify chords is by the root and type. 
So the chords we have built above would be called 
a D major chord (or D major triad), a D minor chord or triad, a D diminished chord (or triad) and a D maugmented chord (or triad).

## inversions


We have seen how we form chords by stacking thirds. 
But in this section we will see that chords can appear in different forms, 
some of which we call inversions. 


We know from previous sections that a C major chord includes the notes C, E and G, 
but this doesn't mean that it has to just have a C, an E a third above it, and a G a fifth above it. 
A C major chord can have Cs, Es and Gs in any octave and in any combination.

### inversions

Depending on which note is the lowest in the chord (we call this being *in the bass*), 
we say that the chord is in a different **inversion**. 

- when the root is in the bass, the chord is in root position
- when the third is in the bass, the chord is in first inversion
- when the fifth is in the bass, the chord is in second position

Look back at the examples of C major chords above, and work out which ones are 
in root posiiton, first inversion, and second inversion. 


### labelling 

We will label the different inversions with the letters a, b, and c. 

a for root position,
b for first inversion,
c for second inversion. 

Together with the roman numerals we learnt about earlier, 
this gives us a system where we can identify what each chord is and what inversion it is in.
