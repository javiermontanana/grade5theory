--- layout: chapter title: What is a Chord ---

Harmony is the mixing together of different sounds to form more complex musical ideas. When we talk about harmony, we usually talking about different notes happening at the same time.

Chords are different notes sounded at the same time. In most Western classical music, chords are built by stacking thirds on top of each other, so we would start with a certain note, then add a note a third above it and then add one a third above that.

This would give us a 3-note chord, also known as a triad. Triads are the most fundamental kind of chord, and most of western classical harmony is built upon them. In this chapter, we will only be dealing with 3-note chords, and I will use the words chord and triad pretty much interchangeably.

Notes in a triad

As we have built out triad by stacking two thirds on top of each other, the interval between the bottom note and the top note of our chord will be a fifth.

We call the bottom note of the chord the root (this is the note we build our chord upon). We call the other notes the third and the fifth, because they are an interval of a third and a fifth above the root.
