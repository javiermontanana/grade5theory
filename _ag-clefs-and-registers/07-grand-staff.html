Of the common clefs, treble and bass clef are the ones that place the staff on the two more extreme registers. The piano, as well as other insturments with a wide range, uses something called a grand staff.

The grand staff consists of 2 staves joined together by braces at the start, with the top staff in treble clef and the bottom one in bass clef. This allows us to write a very wide range by making used of both clefs. In the case of the piano and other keyboard instruments, the bottom staff generally contains the notes played by the left hand, and the top staff is for the right hand.

At the lower end of the upper staff, or at the higher end of the lower staff, there is only the need for one ledger line, for writing middle C.

8ve signs

When the music goes so high that it becomes impractical to write even in treble clef, or too low even for bass clef, we can use a sign to indicate that certain notes should be played an 8ve higher or lower than they are written. This sign consists of an 8, or sometimes 8ve or 8va above or below the staff, with a dotted line to show what notes it applies to. To clarify that the 8ve sign no longer applies, sometimes the word loco is used.
