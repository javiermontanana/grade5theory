Of the common clefs, treble and bass clef are the ones that place the staff on the two more extreme registers. The piano, as well as other keyboard instruments and the harp, uses something called a grand staff.

The grand staff consists of 2 staves joined together by braces at the start, with the top staff in treble clef and the bottom one in bass clef. This allows us to write a very wide range by making used of both clefs. In the case of the piano and other keyboard instruments, the bottom staff generally contains the notes played by the left hand, and the top staff generally contains the notes played by the right hand.

At the lower end of the upper staff, or at the higher end of the lower staff, there is only the need for one ledger line, for writing middle C. Any note lower than middle C can be written on the bottom staff, and any note higher than middle C can be written on the top staff.

8ve signs

When the notes are so high that they become impractical to write even in treble clef, or too low even for bass clef, we can use a sign to indicate that certain notes should be played an octave higher or lower than they are written. This sign consists of an 8, or sometimes 8ve or 8va above or below the staff, with a dotted line to show which notes it applies to. To clarify that the 8ve sign no longer applies, sometimes the word loco is used.

The grand staff can be a very useful device for writing music in a concise way. Very often it is useful to take a score that uses many staves if written out in full and condense it into a grand staff so that it can be played on the piano. This is known as a piano reduction. Often, certain aspects of the music will be omitted or modified so that it is playable on a piano.
