--- layout: chapter title: The Clef Moves the Staff ---

By seeing that middle C is below the stave in treble clef and above it in bass clef, you can see that the notes that fall on the stave are higher in treble clef and lower in bass clef.

In this section, I will show you that you can think of this as the clef moving the stave to a higher or lower register.

Imagine that instead of a 5-line staff, we had many more. With so many lines, we are able to write a very wide range of pitches without using any ledger lines. On this staff, I will place a G clef, a C clef, and an F clef.

Here, the 3 clefs still represent the same pitches as usual: so the C clef represents middle C, the G clef is the G above (that's why it's a fifth above) and the F clef is the F below (that's why it's a fifth below).

Now let's write on this staff the three pitches that the clefs represent. We can write them all without ledger lines.

Now, instead of using all these staff lines, we can use just 5 of them, with the appropriate clef.
