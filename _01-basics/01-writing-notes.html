A note is the smallest unit of sound we have in music. A note is a sound that has a certain duration and a certain pitch.

Notes are represented by these kinds of shapes:

Which consist of a circle, called the notehead, and some other bits. Those other bits, and whether the notehead is coloured in tell us about the duration of the note, and we will learn about that later.

The note is placed on something called the staff in such a way that the placement of the notehead on the staff can tell us the pitch of the note. The pitch of a note means how high or low it sounds.

The Staff

The staff consists of 5 horizontal lines, and it is there so that we can write notes in it allowing us to see the position and thus the pitch.

The staff acts like a kind of grid so that we can see which notes are higher/lower and how much higher/lower they are.

Notes on the staff can be on a line or on a space.

Treble clef and note names

The staff allows us to write notes and see which ones are higher than others, but we still don't know what notes (what pitch) they are. In fact, we can't say what they are until we have something called a clef.

and it tells us that the note on the second staff-line is a G. It does this because the treble-clef symbol represents a G, and by placing the start of the loop on the second line, it indicates that the note on the second line is G.

Note names

Notes are named after the letters A-G, so there are 7 note-names. B is one step higher that A, C is one step higher than B, and so on. When we get to G, the next one is A again, and we repeat.

Now we can name all the notes on the staff in treble clef.

Ledger Lines

The staff allows us to write a few notes, but if we try to go too high or too low we run out of space.

In order to write notes that are above or below the staff, we use something called ledger lines. Ledger lines are little lines that we draw to extend the staff, just for a single note, when we need to write a note that falls outside the staff. We basically pretend that the staff has more than five lines, by just putting them in for the notes where they are required, and we keep naming them the same way.

As we will see later, in a different clef, the note-names for the notes on each staff position will be different.
