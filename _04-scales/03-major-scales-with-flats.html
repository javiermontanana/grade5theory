In the previous section we formed a lot of different major scales, all with sharps. In this section we will form all of the major scales which have flats.

We will start again from C major, and this time we will form a new scale such that the upper tetrachord of our new scale is the lower tetrachord of C major. This is the other way round to what we did in the previous section.

F will be note 1 of our new scale, so it will be an F major scale. If we use just the notes from C major we have this:

This is not quite an F major scale, but it's very close! We just need to lower the B to a B♭.

F major

Now we have all the tones and semitones in the right place, so we have an F major scale.

We can keep doing this to each new scale that we make: creating a new major scale by starting on note 4 of the previous scale (or a perfect fifth below) and flattening note 4 of the new scale (which is note 7 of the old scale).

If we keep repeating this, we obtain the following major scales.
