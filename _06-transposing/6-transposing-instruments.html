--- layout: chapter title: Transposing instruments ---

For some instruments, the pitch of the notes they produce is different to that which is written in their music. These are called transposing instruments.

An instrument is said to be in a certian pitch if when they play the note notated as C, the note that sounds is that pitch.

For example, when a Clarinet in B♭ plays a C, the note that sounds is a B♭, a major second lower than the notated note. So the clarinet in B♭ transposes down by a major 2nd; it sounds a major 2nd lower than it is written.

Why are there transposing instruments?

Transposing instruments are useful because they let players keep the same fingerings when switching between similar instruments. For example, a clarinetist can move between A and B♭ clarinet, or an oboist can switch to cor anglais, and the same note on the page will correspond to the same fingering.

This is especially useful for trumpets and horns. Because of the way brass instruments historically worked, certain music would fit better on instruments tuned to particular pitches. Horn and trumpet players had to switch instruments often, and the music was written so they could play what they saw on the page no matter which instrument they were holding.

Nowadays, there are not so many different types of trumpet and horn in use. The horn in F and trumpet in B♭ are the most common, so players switch much less often, but transposing notation remains a useful and familiar convention.

Which transposing instruments

For the grade 5 theory exam, you need to be familiar with the following transposing insturments:

  • Clarinet in B♭: sounds a major 2nd lower than written
  • Clarinet in A: sounds a minor 3rd lower than written
  • Trumpet in B♭: sounds a major 2nd lower than written
  • Horn in F: sounds a perfect 5th lower than written
  • Cor Anglais in F: sounds a perfect 5th lower than written

Transposing instruments at the octave

In addition to the ones above, there are some instruments which sound an octave lower or higher than written. These are said to be transposing at the octave. You need to know two of these:

  • the piccolo sounds an octave higher than written
  • the double bass sounds an octave lower than written

These instruments are in C. So when they play C, the note that sounds is C, but it is a different C.
