--- layout: chapter title: Compound Time Signatures ---

Compound time signatures are those with 6, 9, or 12 as the top number, but remember that they still have 2, 3, or 4 beats in a bar.

In the case of compound time signatures, the top number tells us the number of subdivisions in the bar, not the number of beats. In compound time signatures, each beat divides into 3 subdivisions, so:

  • when the top number is 6, we have 2 beats in a bar,
  • when the top number is 9 we have 3 beats in a bar,
  • when the top number is 12 we have 4 beats in a bar.

Let's look at an example:

In 6/8 we have 6 quavers in a bar. Remember that the quavers are not the beats, they are the subdivision of the beats. We have 2 beats in a bar. Each beat is 3 quavers long, so a dotted crotchet long.

In compound time signatures, the beats are always a dotted note value, which can be divided into 3 equal sibdivisions.
