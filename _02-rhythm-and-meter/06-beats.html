The way bars are divided into beats is a very important aspect of rhythm and meter. A lot of what we cover in the next few sections will rely on an understanding of this, so it is a very good idea to practise dividing rhythms into the beats of the bar, like we did with the examples in simple and compound time in the last two sections.

The way the pulse of the beat is felt in a rhythm has implications on which notes are more stressed/emphasised. In general, there is a hierarchy within each bar such that the notes which coincide with the beginning of a beat (we say that these notes are on the beat) are stronger than those which don't. In addition to that, the note on the first beat of the bar (that note which coincides with the beginning of the bar) is generally the strongest.

Differences between seemingly equivalent time signatures

In the examples below we have a bar of 3/4 and a bar of 6/8. A bar of 3/4 and a bar of 6/8 are the same length but, as we now know, they are very different. 3/4 is simple and has 3 beats in a bar whereas 6/8 is compound and has 2 beats in a bar. In both examples the bar contains 6 quavers, but they will be grouped differently in a way that shows the beats. In 6/8 they are grouped in 2 groups of 3, and in 3/4 they will be grouped in 3 groups of 2.

I will use accent signs to show how the notes would be emphasised, with a bolder accent sign on the first note of each bar to show that it is the strongest.

This is not to say that whenever we perform rhythms like these, we try to make these accents obvious. In fact, we often try to avoid them! But even when they are not audible, there is an inherent hierarchy within the structure of the bar which still has implications on how the rhythm is felt. And the structure of the bar (the division into beats) is so essential to the rhythm that these two are considered different rhythms even thought they have exactly the same note values.

In general, the beaming or grouping of quavers and shorter values will reflect the beats, so that, where possible, there will be one beam in each beat. We will look at this in more detail in the next section.
