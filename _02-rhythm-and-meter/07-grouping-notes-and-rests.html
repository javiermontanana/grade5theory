As we saw in the previous section, a series of quavers can be beamed in different ways. There are some rules or best practices that composers and publishers tend to follow to make it easier for perfomers to read and understand the structure of the rhythm on the page. Performers generally feel the pulse of the beats in the music as they perform, and having the notes (and rests) grouped into beats so that the eye can pick up each beat as a separate unit on the page can be very helpful.

In this section we look at those best practices for writing rhythms. There are 3 kinds of decisions that we may need to make that relate to this:

  • how to group beamed notes (quavers and shorter values)
  • writing longer rests as a single value or splitting them into shorter values
  • writing longer notes as a single value or splitting them into shorter values joined by ties

The rules for this get really quite complicated, but an awareness of the general principle behind them (to allow the performer's eye to pick up each beat) can get you a long way towards solving most cases.

More on rests

A rest should always stay within a beat, unless it is for either the first half or the second half of a 4-beat bar, in which case a single rest should be used.

Said another way: use a 2-beat rest for either half of a 4-beat bar, but other than that, always show the start of a new beat with a new rest.

Remember that a whole-bar rest is shown as a semibreve rest, no matter what time signature. Never show the beats in a whole bar of rest.

More on splitting longer notes

If a note starts at the beginning of a beat and lasts for a whole number of beats, we will use a single note value where possible. Of course, if we were going to use ties for this, there wouldn't be any point having longer note values at all!

Summary

If the rules seem very complicated, I think it's fine to have a few rules of thumb which can take you most of the way there, even if it means you can't guarantee that you'll be able to answer every possible question in an exam.

The following rules are relatively easy to memorise and will give you the right answer in the vast majority of cases:

  • always use a new rest for a new beat, except for either half of a 4-beat bar
  • never beam over the middle of a 4-beat bar
  • in compound time, always use a new beam for a new beat
  • in simple time, try to use single note values
  • in compound time, split notes to show the start of a new beat

These rules, along with your intuition and your experience reading music will be enough to solve the vast majority of the exam questions on grouping. If in doubt, remember the reason for these rules: the aim is to help the performer read and understand the music while showing the structure of the rhythm.
