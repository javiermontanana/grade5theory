--- layout: chapter title: Scales 6 - major scales and their key signatures ---

Besides being a useful tool for writing music, key signatures give us a useful shorthand for describing scales and allow us to quickly understand what notes are in each scale. We will say things such as "D major has two sharps" by which we mean that it has 2 sharps in the key signature. If we know the order of sharps, this immedaitely tells us that it has an F♯ and a C♯.

With this we can create a table of all the key signatures and the major scales that they correspond to.

Key signature Major Scale
7 sharpsC♯ major
6 sharpsF♯ major
5 sharpsB major
4 sharpsE major
3 sharpsA major
2 sharpsD major
1 sharpG major
no sharps or flatsC major
1 flatF major
2 flatsB♭ major
3 flatsE♭ major
4 flatsA♭ major
5 flatsD♭ major
6 flatsG♭ major
7 flatsC♭ major

Make sure you understand this table, and how it relates to all the major scales we worked out at the beginning of the chapter. When we move up a row in the table, we are adding a sharp (or taking away a flat) and we go to a new scale which is a perfect fifth higher. By understanding this, you should be able to construct the table from scratch, which will allow you to know the key signature (and hence all the notes) for every major scale.
