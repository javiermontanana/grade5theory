Degrees of the scale

So far, I have been referring to notes of the scale as note 1, note 6, etc. But this is a slightly impropper way of refering to notes of the scale. In music theory we refer to scale degrees. So for example, in G major, G would be scale degree 1, A is scale degree 2, F♯ is scale degree 7 and so on...

In the major and minor scales, each degree also has a name that you need to learn. Below is the list of all the names.

Scale degree number Name
1Tonic
2Supertonic
3Mediant
4Subdominant
5Dominant
6Submediant
7Leading Note or Subtonic

By far the most important scale degrees are 1 and 5, the tonic and the dominant. The dominant is always a perfect fifth abve the tonic. There are a few strategies which can help us remember the other ones.

4 is the subdominant. "Sub" means below, and the subdominant is in the same relation to the tonic as the dominant but from below. The subdominant is a perfect fifth below the tonic.

The mediant (from the latin for being in the middle) is midway between the tonic and the dominant.

The submediant is the "mediant" between the tonic and the subdominant, half way between the two (remember that the subdominant is a fifth below the tonic).

Scale degree 2 is the supertonic. "Super" means above, and it is one step above the tonic.

Scale degree 7 is called the leading notewhen it is a semitone (minor 2nd) away from the tonic. The leading note has a tendency to pull (or lead) to the tonic, as it is so close to it and the tonic is the final arrival point of the music. When it is a tone away from the tonic, scale degree 7 is called the subtonic, because it is below the tonic.
