We have been using the words key and scale without properly defining them. In this section we will go deeper into what these words mean and properly disinguish betweeen the two.

A scale is the set of notes when it is ordered stepwise, ascending or descending. Key is the environment of the music, so that it will have the tonic as it's final arrival point. Music that is in a certain key will generally use notes form the scale(s) of that key, and for the purposes of the grade 5 exam, you are absolutely fine to think of keys in this way.

But as you learn more about how tonal music works, you'll understand that this is not strictly speaking true, and what really defines the key of the music is what note is the music directed towards so that it finds a sense of closure or resolution.

For this, the relationship between the tonic and the dominant is crucial. In particular the way the dominant chord has a need of resolution toward the tonic chord. You'll learn more about chords in a different chapter, but very briefly, the dominant chord is a set of notes which contains the leading note, which must be raised in minor keys so that it is a semitone away from the tonic. happy birthday

The song happy birthday to you is in a major key. Depending on what note we choose to start on, it will be in one key or another, we can sing the song by starting on any note we like, but in every case, as long as we sing it correctly, the last note of the song will always be the tonic.

So for example, here is the second half of happy birthday in G major (by the way, this is the same example from section 7 earlier in the chapter). You can see (as we did earlier) that all the notes belong to G major. We have an F♯, C natural, etc

But try playing it in your instrument and try singing it. Doesn't it also feel like it has to end on the note G? G feels like the tonic, the final arrival point of the music.

Here is the same bit of the song, now in the key of A major.

Here again, all the notes belong to A major. If you check, you will see that we have all the right sharps. But again, try to sing or play the tune with your instrument and you should feel that the music resolves at the tonic, which in this case is A.

What we have done here is something called transposing. We have transposed the song up a major 2nd (major 2nd is the interval from G to A) which just means that we have moved the notes up by a major 2nd. We will learn more about transposing in a different chapter and we will use this same example. But for now you should understand that the tonic is the final arrival point of the music and you should try to perceive this in the way you hear tunes.
