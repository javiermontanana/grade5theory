




# Note values


by value we mean duration 

in this chapter we are going to learn everything to do with rhythm and meter. 

If you have learnt that a crotchet is one beat, you must forget that. 
That is a useful way to start learning about note values, 
but it is not always true that a crotchet is one beat. 








## dots

A dot next to a note adds half of its value to that note. 



N.B.: Not to be confused with staccato dots. 


### double dots

A note can also be "double-dotted" meaning it has 2 dots. 
The second dot adds half of the value of the first dot. 


In principle, there could be more than two dots, 
and each dot will add half of the value of the previous dot, 
but it's unusual to see more than 2. 



## ties 

A tie connects two notes and means that they should be played as a single note 
whose value is the sum of the two. 

So for example, a crotchet tied to another crotchet would be a note that lasts for two crotchets. 

This can be very useful if we want a note to start in one bar and last until the middle of the following bar. Then we will need to use a tie. 

[example]

We will later see that ties are sometimens also used to for notes 
that start in one beat of the bar and go into the next beat. 




Ties look exactly the same as slurs, and can be easily confused. 

Slurs indicate that notes should be played in a smooth and connected way 
but don't affect the duration of the notes. 

Ties must always connect notes of the same pitch, 
whereas slurs can connect notes of different pitch.

# bars and time signatures

Music is organised into bars. 

bars are separated by barlines. 

A bar is a unit of musical time.
The way notes are grouped into bars 
follows some rules governed by the time signature. 


## time signatures 

A time signature consists of two numbers one on top of the other, 
which tell us the length of a bar in a particular piece.

The time signature is placed at the beginning, on the first staff, 
right after the clef and the key signature. 

A time signature tells us how many of what note value 
there will be in each bar 
(later we will see that the time signature can also tell us something
about how the bar is divided into beats). 

### understanding time signatures 

A time signature has two numbers, 
the top number, and the bottom nummber. 

The top number simply tells us how many are in each bar. 
This could be any number. 
if it's a 2, then there are 2 in each bar. 

The bottom number tells us of what. 
The bottom number will always be a power of 2 (either 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 etc...)
and each of these numbers corresponds to a note value, such that 
1 is a semibreve, 2 is a minim, 4 is a crotchet, 8 is quavers, etc...


Common time and cut time:
C = 4/4
cut C = 2/2




So, for example: 
2/4 would tell us that each bar is 2 crotchets long. 
3/4 3 crotchets
4/4 4 crotchets
2/2 2 minims
6/8 6 quavers
9/8 9 quavers


You may have noticed that some of the examples above are the same length as others. 
4 crotchets is equivalent to 2 minims, so a bar of 4/4 and 2/2 are the same length. 
6 quavers is equivalent to 3 crotchets, 

That's true. 
But the time signature tells us more about the meter of the music 
than just how long each bar is, so we will see that there are important differences between those. 


### types of time signatures

In the following sections, we will encounter many different time signatures, 
and we will learn what each one means. 

We can divide time signatures into 2 types: regular and irregular. 

In regular time signatures, the bars will be divided into 2, 3, or 4 beats, 
and those beats are the same length. 
There are two kinds of regualar time signatures: simple and compound. 

We will see that simple time signatures have a 2, 3 or 4 as the top number 
and compound time signatures have a 6, 9 or 12 as the top number,
but the essential difference between simple and compound time 
is that in simple time signatures, the beats are divided into 2 equal parts whereas 
in compound time signatures, the beats are divided into 3 equal parts. 


In irregular time signatures, the way the bar is divided into beats 
doesn't necessarily follow any rules 
and can change from one bar to the next, 
so we won't worry about how many beats are in a bar, 
or how long those beats are, or anyhting like that. 
Irregular time signatures are those whose top number is anything other than
2, 3, 4, 6, 9 or 12



# simple time 

Simple time signatures are those with 2, 3, or 4 as the top number. 
This top number is the number of beats in a bar. 

So the time signature tells us in a very straightforward way how many beats 
there are and how long the beats are. 

For exmaple, 
2/4 has 2 crotchets in a bar. It has 2 beats, and each beat is a crotchet. 
4/8 has 4 quavers in a bar. It has 4 beats in a bar and each beat is a quaver. 

Here we can see the difference between two time signatures which have the same bar-lenght. 
Both 2/4 and 4/8 have a bar that is a minim long (2 crothcets or 4 quavers) 
but 2/4 has 2 beats in a bar and 4/8 has 4 beats in a bar. 
This is a subtle but important difference. 

other examples:

In all simple time signatures, the value of the beat will be a simple note value (not dotted), 
which can be divided into 2 subdivisions. 

eg. 4/4, the beat is a crotchet, which divides into 2 quavers. 

As we mentioned earlier this is what makes these simple time signatures. 
We will see in the next section that, in compound time signatures, 
the beat will be a dotted note value, which will be divided into 3 subdivisions. 


# compound time 

Compound time signatures are those with 6, 9, or 12 as the top nnumber,
**but remember that they still have 2, 3, or 4 beats in a bar.**

In the case of compound time signatures, 
**the top number tells us the number of subdivisions in the bar,** 
**not the number of beats.**

In coumpound time signatures, each beat has 3 subdivisions, 
so when the top number is 6, we have 2 beats in a bar,
when the top number is 9 we have 3 beats in a bar,
and when the top number is 12 we have 4 beats in a bar. 

Let's look at some examples: 

The most common compound time signatures are the ones with 8 as the bottom number: 
6/8, 9/8 and 12/8

In 6/8 we have 6 quavers in a bar. 
Remember that the quavers are not the beats, they are the subdivision of the beats. 
We have 2 beats in a bar. 
Each beat is 3 quavers long, so a dotted crotchet long. 

As we said earlier, in compound time signatures, the beats are always a dotted note value, 
which can be divided into 3 equal sibdivisions. 

In 9/8, we have 3 beats in a bar, 
again, each beat is made up of 3 quavers, so a dotted crotchet long.  

12/8 is similar: 4 beats, each 3 quavers long. 

In 6/4, 
we have 2 beats in a bars. 
Each beat is 3 crotchets long, so a dotted minim long.

# the importance of the concept of beats in a bar 

The way bars are divided into beats is a really important aspect of rhythm and meter. 
A lot of what we cover in the next few sections will be to do with this. 

also: 
The way the pulse of the beat is felt in a rhythm has implications on which 
notes are more stressed/emphasised. 
In general, there is a hierarchy within each bar, 
such that the first beat is the strongest, 
and the notes on the beats are stronger than notes not on the beats.

### differences between seemingly equivalent time signatures 

Now that we have learnt about all the regular time signatures, 
we can see that there are important differences between time signatures 
that might at first seem equivalent.

3/4 and 6/8 both contain 6 quavers or 3 crotchets in a bar, 
but in 3/4 we are in simple time and have 3 beats in a bar, 
and in 6/8 we are in compound time and have 2 beats in a bar. 

This means that we could write the same note values to fill one bar, 
let's say 6 quavers 
But those would be two different rhythms. 
As we have mentioned before, the division of the bar into beats is fundamental, 
so those two will be considered different rhythms.
