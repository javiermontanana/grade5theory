--- layout: chapter title: Compound Time Signatures ---

Compound time signatures are those with 6, 9, or 12 as the top nnumber, but remember that they still have 2, 3, or 4 beats in a bar.

In the case of compound time signatures, the top number tells us the number of subdivisions in the bar, not the number of beats.

In compound time signatures, each beat has 3 subdivisions, so when the top number is 6, we have 2 beats in a bar, when the top number is 9 we have 3 beats in a bar, and when the top number is 12 we have 4 beats in a bar.

Let's look at some examples:

The most common compound time signatures are the ones with 8 as the bottom number: 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8

In 6/8 we have 6 quavers in a bar. Remember that the quavers are not the beats, they are the subdivision of the beats. We have 2 beats in a bar. Each beat is 3 quavers long, so a dotted crotchet long.

In compound time signatures, the beats are always a dotted note value, which can be divided into 3 equal sibdivisions.

In 9/8, we have 3 beats in a bar, again, each beat is made up of 3 quavers, so a dotted crotchet long.

12/8 is similar: 4 beats, each 3 quavers long.

In 6/4, we have 2 beats in a bars. Each beat is 3 crotchets long, so a dotted minim long.
