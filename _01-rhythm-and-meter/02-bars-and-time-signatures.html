Music is organised into bars. bars are separated by barlines. A bar is a unit of musical time. The way notes are grouped into bars follows some rules governed by the time signature.

Time Signatures

A time signature consists of two numbers, one on top of the other, which tell us the length of a bar in a particular piece. The time signature is placed at the beginning, on the first staff, right after the clef and the key signature.

The time signature tells us how many of what note value there will be in each bar. Later we will see that the time signature can also tell us something about how the bar is divided into beats.

Understanding Time Signatures

A time signature has two numbers, the top number, and the bottom nummber. The top number simply tells us how many are in each bar. This could be any number. if it's a 2, then there are 2 in each bar.

The bottom number tells us what note value the top number is counting. The bottom number will always be a power of 2 (either 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 etc...) and each of these numbers corresponds to a note value, such that 1 is a semibreve, 2 is a minim, 4 is a crotchet, 8 is quavers, etc...

So, for example: 2/4 would tell us that each bar is 2 crotchets long. 3/4 3 crotchets 4/4 4 crotchets 2/2 2 minims 6/8 6 quavers 9/8 9 quavers

You may have noticed that some of the examples above are the same length as others. 4 crotchets is equivalent to 2 minims, so a bar of 4/4 and 2/2 are the same length. 6 quavers is equivalent to 3 crotchets, so 6/8 and 3/4 are the same length.

That's true. But later, we will see that the time signature tells us more than just how long each bar is, so we will see that there are important differences between those.

Types of time signatures

In the following sections, we will encounter many different time signatures, and we will learn what they all mean.

We can divide time signatures into 2 types: regular and irregular.

In regular time signatures, the bars will be divided into 2, 3, or 4 beats, and those beats are the same length. There are two kinds of regualar time signatures: simple and compound.

We will see that simple time signatures have a 2, 3 or 4 as the top number and compound time signatures have a 6, 9 or 12 as the top number. But the essential difference between simple and compound is that in simple time signatures, the beats are divided into 2 subdivisions, whereas in compound time signatures, the beats are divided into 3 subdivisions.

In irregular time signatures, the way the bar is divided into beats doesn't necessarily follow any rules and can change from one bar to the next, so we won't worry about how many beats are in a bar, or how long those beats are, or anyhting like that. Irregular time signatures are those whose top number is anything other than 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 or 12.
