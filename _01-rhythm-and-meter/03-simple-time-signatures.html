--- layout: chapter title: Simple Time Signatures ---

Simple time signatures are those with 2, 3, or 4 as the top number. This top number is the number of beats in a bar. So the time signature tells us in a very straightforward way how many beats there are and how long the beats are.

For example:

  • 2/4 has 2 crotchets in a bar. It has 2 beats, and each beat is a crotchet.
  • 4/8 has 4 quavers in a bar. It has 4 beats in a bar and each beat is a quaver.

Here we can see the difference between two time signatures which have the same bar-length. Both 2/4 and 4/8 have a bar that is a minim long (2 crotchets or 4 quavers) but 2/4 has 2 beats in a bar and 4/8 has 4 beats in a bar. This is a subtle but important difference.

In all simple time signatures, the value of the beat will be a simple note value (not dotted), which can be divided into 2 subdivisions. eg., in 4/4, the beat is a crotchet, which divides into 2 quavers.

As we mentioned earlier, that is what essentially makes these simple time signatures. We will see in the next section that, in compound time signatures, the beat will be a dotted note value, which will be divided into 3 subdivisions.

Common time and Cut time

4/4 can also be written as a C, and is also known as common time. 2/2 can also be written as a C cut in half by a vertical line and is also known as cut time. These are just equivalent ways of writing those time signatures.
