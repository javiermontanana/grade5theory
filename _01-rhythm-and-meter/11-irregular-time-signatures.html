--- layout: chapter title: Irregular Time Signatures ---

All the simple and compound time signatures are regular. These are the ones with top numbers 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, or 12. Time signatures with any other top number are irregular.

Here, the very first thing we learnt about what the time signature means still applies: the time signature tells us how many of a certain note value fit in each bar.

For example, 7/4 has 7 crotchets. 5/8 has 5 quavers.

But that is as far as we can go with irregular time signatures. We don't have rules that tell us anything about beats in the bar. The whole point is that in irregular time signatures, the notes can be organised into beats in many different ways, that is why they are called irregular.

In music that uses irregular time signatures, the structure of the bars is sometimes made clear by the beaming or some other way, but it is often not made entirely clear and can leave a lot of room for interpretation.

A 5/8 bar might be 2 quavers + 3 quavers, or 3 quavers + 2 quavers, or 1 quaver + 2 quavers + 2 quavers,
