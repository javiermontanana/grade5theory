--- layout: chapter title: Groupings ---

The grouping of beamed notes (quavers and shorter values), also known as beaming, generally follows certain rules, or best practices. These exist to make it easier for performers to read and understand rhythms.

These types of rules also apply to the grouping of rests. As rests can be split or combined without afftecting how the rhythm sounds, we will try to group them in ways that make the rhythm easier to read and understand.

They are not absolute rules, and composers sometimes break them to show that a certain rhythm should be interpreted as having a beat-structure different to that suggested by the time signture, but you should follow them when answering the rythm question in the grade 5 theory exam.

The general principle behind the rules is that the beams should show the beats of the bar.

This general principle will be enough to solve most cases. The other general rule you should know is that in simple time, it is ok to group together more beats.

grouping in simple time

When there are 2 or 3 beats in a bar it is ok to beam several beats together.

When there are 4 beats in a bar, it is ok to group beats 1 and 2 (the first half of the bar), and it is ok to group beats 3 and 4 (the second half of the bar), but it is not ok to group beats 2 and 3. Each half should remain separate.

For rests, each beat should have a separate rest, except when there are 4 beats in a bar, it is ok to have a single rest for either the first half or the second half of the bar.

Grouping in compound time

Each beat should have a separate beam. If necessary, ties should be used so that the start of a new beat can be seen.

For rests, each beat should have a separate rest, except when there are 4 beats in a bar, it is ok to have a single rest for either the first half or the second half of the bar.

When there are rests for a fraction of a beat. You should show each subdivision.
(according to E Taylor you can combine subdivisions 1 and 2)

The rules for grouping get more complicated, but these are a good start and enough for the grade 5 exam. The principles behind the rules always remain the same: the aim is to aid legibility and to show the inner structure of the rhythm.

Remember that a whole-bar rest is shown as a semibreve rest, whatever the time signature. Never show the beats in a whole bar of rest.
